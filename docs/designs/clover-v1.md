## Problem Statement

Students who learn technical skills on their own (Docker, Linux, Python, JS, DSA, and eventually all of computer science) don't stay consistent. The founder's research (`student-consistency-blockers.md`, `student-self-discipline-time-management.md`) shows a chain: students can't structure free time, they study only when an exam forces them, nobody checks their work between exams, the phone fills the empty time, and they quit when a topic gets hard. A one-month skill takes six months or never finishes. Self-study is about 9% of a first-year engineering student's day.

Clover schedules skill tasks into fixed time windows, **verifies** each task was actually done, and when a task is missed, tells a **witness** the student chose (parent, teacher, elder sibling, mentor). Reels stay possible, but skipping a task window costs something: in v1, visibility to someone the student respects; a friction screen before distracting apps comes in v2. The goal is not to block the phone. The goal is that the task gets done on time, every time, and that "enrolled in Clover" comes to mean "can actually do this skill."

## Demand Evidence

- **Research anchor:** Bergman & Chan's RCT (https://www.povertyactionlab.org/node/2353): automated texts to 1,137 parents about missed assignments, absences and low grades in 22 West Virginia middle and high schools cut course failures by 27% (0.71 vs 0.97 per student) and raised classes attended by 12% (312 vs 278). There was no significant effect on standardized tests. (Figures verified against the J-PAL summary on 2026-10-16. An earlier working-paper draft reported 38% / 17%.) **Population difference, stated as an assumption the pilot tests:** that study covered minors with school-generated alerts to parents. Clover targets adults who choose their own witness. It is unproven whether a self-chosen witness has the same effect.
- **Founder is user #1.** In their words: "the fact is i am not consistent on it... none of my projects failed due to technology or my skills."
- **Behavioral evidence from the founder's own history:** AEGIS (`D:\AI\AEGIS`) is a single-user version of this product, with scheduled task windows, a Chrome blocking extension, a Windows activity monitor with watchdog and tamper detection, penalties, and a LangGraph planning agent. It has 7 commits over 7 months (Feb 26, Mar 1, Mar 30 x2, then a six-month gap to Sep 20 x2) and never reached v1. Enforcement software the founder controlled did not keep the founder consistent. AEGIS never had a second person who would notice.
- **No external demand evidence yet.** No other student, parent, or teacher has been asked. This is the biggest gap.

## Status Quo

- The founder has tried blockers and self-made systems and turned them off or ignored them. The founder's own diagnosis is a lack of consistency, not a lack of tools.
- Market evidence: of 467 people who agreed to use a blocker, 119 kept it running for 10 days (reported by Reclaim, https://reclaim.ai/blog/focus-apps; original study not located). Android/Play policy forbids preventing uninstall via the Accessibility API, and Family Link lets adults end supervision anytime.

Partner-report apps (Covenant Eyes, Accountable2You, $7-17/mo) exist for porn accountability, not study. Study accountability apps don't check whether the work was actually done.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **Student:** an 18-22 year old Indian CS student or self-learner who wants a real skill (starting with Docker) and has failed to stay consistent alone. First instance: the founder.
- **Witness:** a person the student respects and chooses: parent, teacher, elder sibling, mentor. Needs no app, only WhatsApp messages.
- **Wedge:** one track (Docker, about 15 tasks) with real machine-checked verification, plus witness alerts on misses. Docker was picked because no student tool covers it, the founder is learning it now, and it uses the hardest verifier type.

## Constraints

- **Android only in v1.** iOS Screen Time APIs need Apple's FamilyControls entitlement.
- **No Accessibility Service in v1.** It's needed for Reels-specific detection, the friction overlay, and uninstall alerts, and it triggers Play declaration and review.
- **WhatsApp Business API:** a message the business starts (outside a 24h user window) must use a pre-approved template. AI text sent to the witness can only fill template slots.
- **Docker tasks run on a laptop**, so verification needs a laptop-side checker, not just the phone.
- Students are adults. Every form of monitoring is opt-in and chosen by the student.

## Premises

1. The core of v1 is the witness loop: scheduled task, then proof, then an automatic message to the witness on a miss. Friction and blocking come second.
2. "Done" is proved by a per-task verifier behind one contract. The contract has a `type` field; `state_check` is the only type built and specified in v1. `test_suite`, `quiz` and `ai_review` are reserved type names whose schemas are written when each is built. The claim "you'll be skillful" is earned from the verified record. Outcomes:
   - **passed in window:** streak continues, no message
   - **attempted but failed:** supportive note to the student; it appears only in the witness's weekly summary, never as an alert
   - **no attempt by deadline:** the witness is alerted
3. Bypassing the system is made visible, not impossible. In v1 the only thing that triggers a witness alert is **no valid verify by the deadline**. Phone heartbeat gaps are recorded as an informational "app offline" fact and are never a miss on their own (see v1 Behavior Rules).
4. The founder is student #1. AEGIS is a lesson, not a codebase to extend (its ideas carry over; its code does not).
5. The student chooses the witness and opts in. Removing the witness has a **72h cooling-off period, and the witness is notified**. The witness sees **the miss plus app-open minutes during the window** ("Missed Docker lab 7-8 PM. Instagram open 41 min during that window."), never content or browsing history.
6. Messages are AI-generated from the student's context. **Facts come only from the event log; the LLM only does the phrasing.** In v1, witness messages are fully static approved WhatsApp templates with narrow factual slots, and no LLM text. LLM phrasing is for student messages only, behind a programmatic guardrail with a static fallback (see v1 Behavior Rules).

### v1 Behavior Rules

These are defaults chosen during spec review; change any of them at approval.

**Scheduling and progression**
- At setup the student picks recurring windows (default: Mon-Fri, one 60-min window per day, for example 19:00-20:00 IST). One task per window, assigned in track order.
- PASSED or PASSED_LATE: the track advances. MISSED or ATTEMPTED_FAILED: the same task carries to the next window. A task never spans two windows.
- Cadence: one task per weekday. The 15-task Docker track takes about 3 weeks; the 14-day self-test covers about 10 tasks.

**Outcomes and timing (server time decides)**
- In-window status is judged by the **server's receipt time**, never the client timestamp. There is a 15-min grace period after the window closes, to cover network delay.
- The CLI queues a verify if it is offline and sends it when back online. It counts as in-window only if the server receives it before close + grace.
- Outcomes:
  - `PASSED`: all checks pass, received before close + grace.
  - `PASSED_LATE`: all checks pass, received after close + grace but **before the next non-paused scheduled window opens** (a weekend or paused day extends it). It is recorded as a new outcome event that supersedes `MISSED` or `ATTEMPTED_FAILED` for that window (and resets that task's struggling counter) (the `MISSED` event stays in the log). It **advances the track**. The witness was already alerted; the weekly summary marks it "made up". A pass received after the next window opens counts only for that next window.
  - `ATTEMPTED_FAILED`: a valid attempt (defined below) with not all checks passing.
  - `MISSED`: no valid attempt.
  - `PAUSED`: declared before the window opened.
  - `SERVER_OUTAGE`: Slice 1 startup rule (eng D1), see Hosting and scheduler recovery.
- **A valid attempt** (this closes the loophole of running `clover verify` once without doing the work) needs at least one check marked `unique: true` for this task passing (leftover state from earlier tasks can't satisfy it), OR at least 2 verify runs at least 10 min apart inside the window. Runs reported as `environment_error` (Docker daemon down, SDK error) never count. Anything less is `MISSED`. The track loader rejects a task spec with no unique check (eng D8).
- **Two `ATTEMPTED_FAILED` in a row on the same task** send the witness a non-accusatory "struggling" template: "[Name] has attempted [task] twice and is stuck. A word of encouragement may help." It is sent once per task; later failures on that task appear only in the weekly summary. No other alert is sent for failed attempts.

**Heartbeat**
- The Android heartbeat (WorkManager, best-effort) is **informational only**. A gap during a window shows as "app offline" in the student app and the weekly summary. It never sends a witness alert and never overrides a server-recorded pass. During onboarding the app shows guidance on battery-optimization exemption for Xiaomi/Oppo/Vivo/Realme.

**Witness messages**
- **Cadence (v1 default, not configurable):** one instant alert per `MISSED`, sent at close + grace, plus a **weekly summary every Sunday at 20:00 IST**. The summary lists: tasks passed / made up / failed / missed / paused, streak, total app-open minutes during windows, and any "app offline" gaps.
- **Witness states:** `invited → active | declined | opted_out | undeliverable`. Alerts go only to an `active` witness. Replying STOP sets `opted_out` immediately. A delivery failure 2 times in a row sets `undeliverable`. The student's app shows the state at all times. Without an active witness, the student sees a banner: "You are not being witnessed."
- **Removal:** a 72h cooling-off. **Alerts continue during those 72h.** The witness is told at the start ("[Name] will remove you as witness on [date]") and at the end.
- **Leaving Clover:** the same 72h path. The witness is told "[Name] has left Clover". After that, alerts stop, events and usage data are deleted from live data immediately, and they leave backups within 7 days. (Slice 2, eng D2: deletion scope covers all personal data, and deleted IDs are re-applied after any restore; see TODOS.md.)

**Pause days**
- Up to 2 pause days per week, declared in the app or CLI before the window opens. Paused windows send no alert. The weekly summary shows the pause count to the witness.

**Message pipeline**
- Witness templates are fixed factual text with narrow slots only (`{name}`, `{task_title}`, `{window}`, `{app_minutes_line}`, `{date}`). No LLM-generated text reaches the witness in v1.
- Student messages (Slice 3) are LLM-phrased from event facts. The guardrail is a programmatic check:
  1. every number, time and task name in the output must appear in the input facts;
  2. at most 280 characters;
  3. a banned-word list (shame words like lazy, failure, useless);
  4. no mention of the witness's identity.
- If the guardrail fails, the LLM errors, or the call takes more than 5s, the static template is sent instead.

**Accounts and data**
- App login by phone OTP (Firebase Auth). The CLI pairs through a 6-digit, 10-min code shown in the app, which issues a device token. All API calls use bearer tokens. (In Slice 1, before the app exists, `clover init` exchanges a server-side admin secret, set in the server's environment, for a device token; the endpoint returns 403 without it.)
- The LLM receives only event facts and the student's first name. It never receives phone numbers, the witness's identity, or apps outside the tracked list.
- App-usage minutes are kept 90 days; events are kept until account deletion.

**Hosting and scheduler recovery**
- One always-on small VM (for example a Hetzner CX22 or an Oracle Cloud free ARM instance) with SQLite on a persistent disk and a nightly backup to object storage. No sleeping free tiers.
- The scheduler is idempotent and catches up. Every minute, and on startup, it scans for windows past close + grace that have no outcome event, and decides them. No job state lives only in memory.
- **Outage rule, Slice 1 (eng D1):** on the first `run_pass` after the process starts, any window whose close + grace already passed with no outcome event is decided `SERVER_OUTAGE`. That outcome **never alerts the witness**: the task carries over, it appears in the weekly summary as "Clover was down", and `clover admin void <window>` lets the founder settle it. Client clocks are never trusted. Deploys are scheduled outside any window. Uptime ticks and hang detection move to Slice 2 (TODOS.md).
- Nightly backups are kept on a **7-day rotation**, so deleted accounts are gone from backups within 7 days of the deletion. The deletion promise is restated as "deleted from live data immediately and from backups within 7 days".

**WhatsApp provider**
- Meta WhatsApp Cloud API with the founder's own business number from day 1 (not the Twilio sandbox, where witnesses must send a join code and membership expires). Utility templates (Slice 1: miss alert, struggling, weekly summary, consent confirmation reply; Slice 2: removal and leave notices) are submitted **during the manual pilot, before the 7-day build clock starts**. There is no cold opt-in template; consent always starts with the witness's own message. Until they're approved, the founder forwards alerts by hand from the server log. **Verify current Meta template and pricing rules before building.**

## Success Criteria

- **Founder self-test (primary):** over 14 days of the built system (Slice 1 is enough; Slice 2 if shipped) with a real witness, on-time completion is measured two ways, and **both must pass**:
  - strict: `PASSED` / all scheduled windows, **counting `PAUSED` as not done**, at least 70%;
  - lenient: `PASSED` / non-paused windows, at least 80%.
  - Report the pause count alongside. `SERVER_OUTAGE` windows are excluded from both.
  - **Pass/fail:** the self-test passes if both thresholds above are met **and** strict completion is at least 20 points above the final Phase A baseline from the manual pilot. If Phase A was 60% or higher, strict completion must be at least 85% instead of the +20 rule. There's no extension: 14 days, one verdict.
- **The witness actually reacts:** at least one miss produces a real-world response from the witness (a call or a message), recorded.
- **No fake passes:** zero task passes the founder knows were gamed.
- **External pull:** at least 3 of 5 classmates asked are willing to name a specific witness; at least 1 starts the track.
- **Ship gate:** the first real witness alert fires from the **Slice 1** system (not manually) within 7 days of starting to build. Slices 2-3 have no gate. They start only after Slice 1 has fired a real alert.

## The Assignment

**Before writing any Clover code, within 48 hours:** ask one specific person to be your witness. It could be your elder sibling, a parent, or a mentor whose opinion you actually care about. Say it in person or on a call: "If I skip a Docker task, you'll get a message. You don't have to do anything except notice."

Then run a **two-phase manual pilot** (one Docker task per day in a fixed evening window, scheduled in your calendar):
- **Phase A, days 1-4, no witness:** do the tasks alone and write down on-time or not each day. This is your **baseline**.
- **Phase B, the 7 days after Phase A ends, with witness:** proof is a screenshot of the verifying command (`docker ps`, `curl localhost:8080`) posted to a WhatsApp chat with your witness **before the window closes**. The witness's only job is to notice silence.
- **Baseline length:** if Phase A is 60% or higher after 4 days, extend it to 7 days. Phase B always starts the day after Phase A ends and runs 7 days. "A" below means the final Phase A rate.
- **Decision after 7 days of Phase B.** Every case maps to exactly one outcome:

| Case | Build Slice 1 | Rethink before coding | Extend Phase B by 7 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal (A < 60%) | B ≥ 70% **and** B − A ≥ 30 | B < 50% **or** B − A < 15 | everything else |
| High baseline (A ≥ 60%) | B ≥ 85% **and** B's last 3 days ≥ its first 3 | B < 70% | everything else |

- **After the extension, the verdict is final**, judged on the combined 14-day Phase B. Build only if the Build column holds, with a relaxed gap: B ≥ 70% and B − A ≥ 20 (normal), or B ≥ 85% (high baseline). Otherwise rethink. No second extension.
- "Rethink" means the witness alone didn't move you, so ask why before writing code (wrong witness? tasks too big? wrong time of day?).

During the same week, ask 5 classmates one question: "Would you let someone you respect be told when you skip practice? Who would it be?" Count how many name a specific person.

If Phase B beats Phase A, you've proved the core before writing a line of code. If it doesn't, you've learned that in a week instead of seven months.

### R3-1 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> A single missing uptime_tick anywhere in the 75-minute span turns the window into an outage window, and if no queued verify arrives it is decided SERVER_OUTAGE with no witness alert and exclusion from both success metrics. Routine events such as a GitHub Actions deploy on push to main, a process restart, or one slow minute would void accountability for a window in which the student simply did nothing, and the student never tried to reach the server. The fix for false accusations creates a silent excuse path that undermines the core witness loop and inflates the self-test result.

**Remedy**

> Apply SERVER_OUTAGE only when the outage could have blocked the student: e.g. the CLI reports a queued verify created during the gap, or the gap covers a material share of the window or the close + grace boundary (state a threshold). Otherwise decide the window normally. Also forbid deploys during scheduled windows and report outage-excluded windows in the self-test results.

Slice 2 'witness setup and states in the app' would follow the architecture and reintroduce the business-initiated opt-in the change removed.
