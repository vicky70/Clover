# Why Students Are Not Consistent: Top 5 Blockers

Research-backed reasons why students (especially in India) struggle to learn skills consistently.

---

## 1. Weak Self-Management (biggest factor)

Students don't know how to plan, set goals, or manage their time on their own. Colleges rarely teach this; they give a timetable instead.

- In a study of 463 college students, self-regulated learning explained **42% of procrastination**. Time and study environment had the strongest negative link with procrastination.
- Source: https://ext.newnonmun.com/article=8980

## 2. Exam-Only Motivation

Indian students learn mainly to pass exams. When the exam ends, the learning stops too.

- Exam-centric culture over learning is one of the key problems named in Indian engineering education.
- Students mostly want good grades, not continuous learning.
- Sources:
  - https://www.scribd.com/document/305191119/Report
  - https://iamayusshkhurana.notion.site/Difficulties-and-challenges-that-students-experience-in-computer-science-engineering-in-India-1-b56fb66cacd4484f9082c2fc2d542094

## 3. Slow or No Feedback

When nobody checks their work quickly, students lose motivation. This links directly to the teacher-workload problem.

- A study that gave feedback at 1, 3, 7, 10, or 14 days after submission found that turnaround time and the feedback comments both affect motivation. The content of the feedback had the biggest impact.
- In another study, delayed feedback hurt motivation and performance, while immediate corrective feedback improved both.
- Sources:
  - https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/the-impact-of-timely-formative-feedback-on-university-student-mot/
  - https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/191752

## 4. Phone and Social Media Distraction

- An Indian study of 277 university students found a direct link between time on social media and the amount of procrastination.
- A survey of 5,000 Indian families found students spend about **4 hours a day** on screens, and around **68%** of that time is not related to study.
- Sources:
  - https://sciencespot.co.uk/?p=30803
  - https://neeleshmisra.com/read/the-silent-distraction-in-every-home-during-board-exams/amp_articleshow/129977747.html

## 5. Topic Feels Hard, or Tasks Feel Pointless

- Most MOOC dropouts gave reasons like not enough time, difficult subject matter, and unchallenging activities.
- MOOC completion rates are around **7%** worldwide.
- Students procrastinate more when the content doesn't feel relevant, because that lowers how much they value the task and raises its emotional cost.
- Sources:
  - https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/2743
  - https://www.ecampusnews.com/?p=55950
  - https://education.msu.edu/rldl/2021/2021/04/publication-on-procrastinate-in-online-courses

## How They Connect

1. Students can't plan on their own (**Blocker 1**)
2. So they wait for exams to push them (**Blocker 2**)
3. Between exams, nobody checks their work (**Blocker 3**)
4. The phone fills the empty time (**Blocker 4**)
5. When a topic gets hard, they quit and restart later (**Blocker 5**)

Result: a 1-month skill takes 6 months or more, or never gets finished.
